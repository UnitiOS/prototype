Transcript, @rival/stock-moves v1. Demonstration content — none of this is real.

This module exists to be a fair test, so the conditions it was written under
matter more than what it says.

It was written against @uniti/core@v2 and nothing else. No catalogue is pinned.
No class belonging to another module is named. The words were chosen to be
different from @demo/inventory's wherever a choice existed: movement became
Movement, quantity became qty_delta, ingredient became item, happened_on became
happened, location became kept_at, and the whole thing lives under `rv:` rather
than `inv:`.

The one thing it shares with @demo/inventory is the three answers core asked
for. Both say what their amount is, what it affects, and when it happened.

The range of `item` is left at Resource. @demo/inventory narrowed the same
binding to Ingredient, which tied it to a bakery; this one does not, so a
textile mill could install it and the log would say a bolt of cloth is a
Resource. Neither choice is wrong — they are different modules for different
appetites, and the role is what lets an application stop caring which it got.

`kept_at` answers no role, deliberately. It is here so that swapping a module
has a visible cost as well as a benefit: an application grouping by
@demo/inventory's location cannot reach this, and the fit report has something
true to say instead of a clean bill of health that would prove nothing.
