Transcript, @demo/stock v3. Demonstration content — none of this is real.

This version is the experiment v2 was written to make possible.

The bakery is imagined to have decided that @demo/inventory is not good enough
and to have moved to @rival/stock-moves, a module by somebody else. The two
share nothing but core: different class names, different slot names, a
different prefix, and an author who had never seen the bakery's catalogue.

What was changed to make the move: one line of `imports`.

What was changed in the rules: nothing. Not a word. The file below `classes:`
is byte-identical to v2's, and a test asserts it rather than trusting this
paragraph.

The numbers come out the same, which is the result worth having — but it is
worth being clear about why they do. They are not the same numbers because the
system translated anything. They are the same because both modules answered
core's three questions about their own data, and the rule was written against
the questions rather than against either set of answers.

Two things were given up, and both are in the fit report rather than buried:

Location. @demo/inventory had one; @rival calls its own idea of a place
`kept_at` and it answers no role. So a rule grouping by location could not have
survived this move. This application never grouped by one, which is the only
reason the move was clean — and that was luck the first time and is a thing to
check deliberately from now on.

The old statements. Every fact the bakery recorded under `inv:` is still in the
log, untouched. Nothing was migrated and nothing was deleted; they are simply
no longer read, because no module in this closure declares their predicates.
Bringing them back would be an alignment module, not a migration.
