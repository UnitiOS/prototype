Transcript, @uniti/transaction v1.

Written by hand by the core keeper, as core is: the studio offers no step that
declares a role, because a role is shared vocabulary every module may fill and
is not something a module author adds in passing.

WHAT PROMPTED IT

The empty-store test of 4-6 October 2026 (UJI_LINGKUNGAN_KOSONG.md). A rental
module recorded one row per item rented and nothing for the rental itself, so
when a second module recorded the deposit a customer paid for a rental, there
was nothing to point at: Citra's deposit would have had to name one of the three
lines of her rental. And because no module said who a customer was, the deposit
module declared a customer of its own.

WHAT WAS DECIDED (DESIGN_NOTES.md section 15, PUSTAKA_UMUM.md)

- A transaction is a role, not a class. Each module keeps its own class for its
  own transactions, so the owner of every class stays clear; the role is what
  lets them be found and pointed at by one word.
- A transaction is an event, so it carries the day it happened (core's
  `moment`), and it names the party it was with (`transaction_counterparty`, a Party from
  @uniti/party@v1). Customer and supplier are places in a transaction, not kinds
  of party.
- A line names its transaction (`line_transaction`). A line that moves an amount of an
  item fills core's Flow as well; this module adds nothing for that.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO YET

No mechanism reads these roles. Grouping a line by something its transaction
says (the lines of all of Budi's rentals) needs the compiler to reach one record
further, which is planned next. Until then a line that fills Flow carries its
own day, the same as its transaction's.
