Provenance note for business/draft.yaml — Marlow Gelato, the v1 draft.

There was no conversation. The interview is deferred for this PoC (DECISIONS.md,
29 Aug), so the map was authored by hand from business/profile.md and from
nothing else, and this note stands where a transcript would. It records what was
read, what was decided where the profile does not decide, and what was rejected,
so that "why does the map say this" is answerable from a file rather than from
whoever wrote it.

Nothing outside profile.md was used as a source. DECISIONS.md and NEXT.md were
read as instructions about how to author, never as facts about the shop.


WHAT BECAME WHAT
----------------

The profile's own read order was followed: section 4 things became classes,
section 5 attributes became slots, section 6 relationships became slots whose
range is another class, and section 7 movements became classes. The facts are
the count of 31 August and the standing master data the shop already held on
that evening.

  section 1         two named people, and which two flavours are seasonal
  section 3         the three mix formulas, named
  section 1, 3, 8   descriptions on the classes, in the shop's own words
  section 4         nine classes: Material, Supplier, Flavour, Base, MixBatch,
                    Pan, Location, StockCount, Sale
  section 5         the attribute slots, each carrying the profile's unit where
                    the profile fixes one; and the attributes it states values
                    for — a scoop price, whether a flavour is in rotation, the
                    base of one flavour, one shelf life, one pack, one price
  section 6         eleven slots whose range is a class, listed below
  section 7         five more classes under one parent, listed below
  section 8         the five places stock sits, each with its temperature
  section 10        six stated_rule annotations, verbatim, on the slot each rule
                    is about
  section 11        two readings of "the vanilla" as two slots on one subject;
                    two meanings of "batch" as MixBatch and GoodsReceived
  section 12        gaps, kept as gaps: no slot was written for any of them
  section 13        the count itself — nineteen materials and ten flavours

176 facts, and every one of them comes from a section above:

  section 13        125    nineteen materials, ten flavours, three units
  section 8          10    five locations, a name and a temperature each
  section 5          36    sixteen prices, sixteen rotations, one flavour base,
                           and three attributes of two materials
  section 3           3    the white, chocolate and sorbet bases
  section 1           2    Marta and Dan

Three classes are not in section 4 and are there because section 6 or the
avoidance of any_of requires them:

  Person      section 6 states "Mix batch was made by a person", and a
              relationship's range has to be a class
  Unit        section 6 states "Material is measured in Unit"
  StockItem   the common parent of Material and Pan, so that "Stock count
              counted Material or Pan" is one class-ranged slot

Section 6, relationship by relationship, so the coverage can be checked:

  Flavour is made from Base          flavour_base        -> Base
  Mix batch is a batch of Base       mix_base            -> Base
  Mix batch was made by a person     mix_made_by         -> Person
  Pan holds Flavour                  pan_flavour         -> Flavour
  Pan was filled from Mix batch      pan_mix             -> MixBatch
  Pan sits in Location               stock_location      -> Location
  Material is bought from Supplier   material_supplier   -> Supplier
  Material is measured in Unit       material_unit       -> Unit
  Stock count counted Material/Pan   count_of            -> StockItem
  Stock count happened at Location   count_location      -> Location
  Sale was of Flavour                sale_flavour        -> Flavour

Three of the sixteen class-ranged slots carry a fact: stock_location,
material_unit and flavour_base. The other thirteen are in the table below.


WHO WOULD HAVE WRITTEN THESE FACTS
----------------------------------

All 176 facts are one act: what Marta and Dan stated on the evening of
31 August 2025, described to an interviewer and sealed. That is the onboarding
writer named by the 30 Aug decision — the interview and `seal` — so they belong
in one intent and there is nothing here a generated form or an agent would have
written instead. No daily entry, no correction, no derived total.

The test applied while writing: if a fact would have arrived on a Friday from an
invoice, on a Sunday from Dan's paper log, or as a correction, it does not
belong in this draft.

Cream's £2.80 is the one fact that has to be argued rather than asserted.
Section 5 says the price comes from the invoice, and an invoice is a Friday
thing. What is recorded here is not an invoice line but what Marta says a carton
costs when she is asked at onboarding — standing master data, the same kind of
answer as a pack size or a shelf life. The first delivery to disagree with it
arrives as a GoodsReceived through a generated form, and it corrects this fact
rather than repeating it.


CHOICES THE PROFILE DOES NOT MAKE
---------------------------------

Each of these was underdetermined by profile.md. The plainest option was taken,
and the alternative is recorded so it can be taken back.

 1. Section 7's movements are five classes, not nine and not none.
    Four of the nine rows are already section 4 things — "base made" is a
    MixBatch, "pan filled" is a Pan, "scoop sold" is a Sale, "stock counted" is a
    StockCount — so only five are new: GoodsReceived, PanMoved, PanPulled,
    ThrownOut, TastingGiven. They share one StockMovement parent carrying what
    section 7's table states: what moved, how much, out of where, into where,
    when, and who recorded it.
    Rejected: omitting section 7 entirely. The done condition names sections 4
    and 6 only, but it also sets a floor — at least what an ERP inventory module
    holds — and an inventory module with no stock movements does not clear it.
    Also rejected: one class with a movement_kind enum. The three movements
    nobody records are three of these five, and as enum values they would be
    three strings rather than three things a reader can see are empty.

 2. StockItem carries stock_location, so a material and a pan use one slot to say
    where they sit.
    Rejected: separate material_location and pan_location slots, leaving
    StockItem with nothing in it. Section 8 states location as a property of
    stock in general ("one place stock sits"), and a parent that exists only to
    be the range of one slot is an artefact of avoiding any_of rather than
    something the business says.

 3. The pan-table facts state no confidence at all, and neither does any fact
    outside section 13.
    Section 13 gives a Confidence column to the nineteen materials and to
    nothing else, so the six estimated quantities record `low`, the thirteen
    counted ones record `high`, and every other fact records NULL.
    Rejected: marking the flavour rows `high` from section 13's prose, "the
    freezers were counted pan by pan". It is true, but the business wrote a
    confidence beside nineteen numbers and not beside these, and a default claims
    more than anyone said — the same reasoning as the 30 Aug decision.

 4. Location, Unit, Base and Person receive a name fact; Supplier, Pan,
    MixBatch, StockCount, Sale and the five movements receive nothing.
    The profile names five locations (section 8), three units (section 13's Unit
    column), three bases (section 3, listed again in section 4) and two people
    (section 1), so those thirteen entities are named as well as pointed at.
    Supplier is the loud absence: section 4 says there are four and names none,
    so there is no Supplier individual and no material says who sells it.
    Rejected: leaving the named ones as bare URIs minted only because a fact
    points at them. Then "38 litres in the chiller" would be readable nowhere
    but in the URI, and a URI is not a name.

 5. The flavour called Dark chocolate is uniti:dark_chocolate_flavour, while the
    eight kilograms of chocolate in the dry store is uniti:dark_chocolate.
    The shop uses one phrase for two things, exactly as section 11 describes for
    "the vanilla" and "batch". It is the only collision in section 13.
    Rejected: prefixing every URI with its class — uniti:material_sugar,
    uniti:flavour_vanilla. Tidier, and it hides that the collision happened.

 6. A name slot is `identifier: true` only where section 4 names one field that
    identifies the thing: Material, Supplier, Flavour, Base, Location, Unit,
    Person. Pan is not — section 4 says the number is written on tape and section
    12 says numbers are reused, so pan_number is an ordinary slot. MixBatch,
    StockCount and Sale are identified by two fields each, so none of their slots
    is an identifier either.
    Rejected: no identifiers anywhere. That would drop the one place the profile
    is explicit about identity, and it would lose the finding that four of the
    nine things have no single identifier the shop can state.

 7. The six flavours with a sticker over them carry a name, a rotation of false
    and a scoop price, and no quantity.
    Section 13 names peach, tiramisu, hazelnut, raspberry sorbet, black sesame
    and gingerbread as having had no stock, and section 5 states a price and a
    rotation for a flavour as such, not for a flavour that happens to be in the
    cabinet. A flavour off the menu board still has a price on the menu board.
    Rejected: a quantity of zero. Nobody weighed them; "had no stock" is a
    sentence, and 0.0 kg is a measurement. Also rejected: leaving them out
    entirely, which would lose six of the sixteen recipes the shop has.

 8. Section 10's shrinkage rule is a stated_rule on count_quantity, and there is
    no shrinkage slot.
    The month-end count is where section 9 says the shop settles its numbers, so
    the threshold hangs there. v1 carries no derived rule, per the 29 Aug
    decision, so how shrinkage is computed is not in this map at all — only that
    above five percent means something is wrong.
    Rejected: a shrinkage slot carrying Marta's arithmetic in prose. It would be
    a derived quantity in a version that is supposed to have none, and it would
    spend the definition change before it happens.

 9. The rule on flavour_kilograms still says "below five litres" while the slot
    is in kilograms. Left as it stands: section 12 records that nobody has ever
    said which unit that rule means, and rewriting it in kilograms would answer
    the question on the shop's behalf.

10. flavour_base is stated for pistachio and for nothing else.
    Section 5 gives one example of which base a flavour uses — "pistachio uses
    white" — and that is the whole of what the profile says about the recipe
    book's fifteen other entries.
    Rejected: reading the names. Dark chocolate and stracciatella all but
    announce the chocolate base, and the three sorbets announce the sorbet base,
    and a map that wrote those down would be answering from the flavour's name
    rather than from the recipe book. It is the most tempting invention in the
    whole profile, and if it were made, nothing downstream could tell the
    inferred fifteen from the stated one.

11. Three of the five locations are named and pointed at by nothing.
    The blast freezer, the storage freezer and the display cabinet exist in the
    map with a name and a temperature and hold no stock, because section 13
    reported the twenty-one pans per flavour rather than per freezer.
    Rejected: naming only the chiller and the dry store, the two the count
    pointed at. Section 8 is a five-row table the shop states as master data,
    and a location master holding two of five would be the map's gap rather
    than the shop's. The pans-per-location numbers are in section 13 —
    twelve on display, nine in storage — but as counts per flavour, which is
    the untidiness choice 12 below keeps.

12. Where a flavour's pans sit is folded into the slot names,
    flavour_pans_on_display and flavour_pans_in_storage, rather than being a
    dimension of one quantity. This is the untidiness the 30 Aug decision
    accepted on purpose, and it is why the three empty locations stay empty:
    the map has no slot that would say "this many pans of vanilla, in the
    storage freezer".

13. Units and bases are written in the shop's lower case — litre, kg, piece,
    white, chocolate, sorbet — and locations, materials, flavours and people in
    the capitalised form their own tables use. Each name is copied from the
    table the profile states it in.
    Rejected: normalising all of them one way. The capitalisation is the
    profile's, and copying it keeps the fact traceable to the row it came from.

14. Every flavour carries £4.20 except peach and gingerbread at £5.50.
    Section 5's price column reads "£4.20, seasonal £5.50" and section 1 names
    the two seasonal flavours, so the seasonal price attaches to those two and
    the other fourteen take the plain one.
    Rejected: leaving the seasonal price off, on the grounds that section 5
    calls the column Typical. The shop states one menu price and one seasonal
    price, and a menu with no prices on it is not what the profile describes.


WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT WRITTEN
---------------------------------

Section 12's gaps are gaps. There is no slot for density per flavour, for actual
overrun, for actual scoop weight, or for a supplier lot number, and there is no
history before 1 September 2025. A map that answers those would have invented
business.

No pan is named. Section 13 weighed twenty-one of them and wrote down ten
totals, so twenty-one individuals would mean twenty-one invented weights, and
section 12 says the numbers on the tape are reused. Nothing about a supplier is
named either: section 4 counts four and names none.

Twenty-six of the forty-five slots receive no fact. That is what the shop had
not said by the evening of 31 August, and each one is empty for a reason the
profile gives:

  supplier_name              §4 counts four suppliers and names none, so there
                             is no Supplier individual to carry a name
  material_supplier          §4, the same: nothing exists to point at, and §13
                             records no supplier beside any material

  pan_number                 §12 says the numbers are written on tape and
                             reused, so a number is not an identity
  pan_flavour                §13 reports the twenty-one pans as ten totals per
  pan_mix                    flavour, so no pan is an individual; the per-pan
  pan_filled_kilograms       facts in §5 are Dan's paper log, which §9 says
  pan_pulled_kilograms       reaches the system on a Sunday — after this seal
  pan_filled_on

  mix_base                   §13: "No mix batches were in the chiller — the
  mix_made_by                count was done on a Sunday and the last production
  mix_litres                 run was Friday." There is nothing to describe
  mix_pasteurised_on

  count_of                   §13 states what the count found, not the count as
  count_location             a thing that can be pointed at; a quantity hangs on
  count_taken_on             the thing counted (30 Aug). §2 puts the first count
  count_quantity             recorded as an event at the month end, after this

  sale_flavour               §9: the till is read the next morning, so the first
  sale_date                  sale fact is dated 1 September and learned on the
  sale_scoops                2nd. §13 is a balance, not a day's trading

  movement_of                §7 and §9: a delivery is learned on the Friday and
  movement_quantity          production on the Sunday, and §12 says nothing
  movement_out_of            before 1 September is recoverable. Onboarding
  movement_into              states a balance, never the movements that
  movement_happened_on       produced it — which is the point: three of the nine
  movement_recorded_by       movements are never recorded at all
  received_from

Four more slots are stated for one subject each and empty for the rest, for the
same kind of reason: flavour_base for pistachio (choice 10 above),
material_shelf_life_days for fresh milk, material_pack_size and
material_pack_price for double cream. Section 5 states one example of each and
the profile never states a second, so eighteen materials have no pack, no price
and no shelf life, and fifteen flavours have no base.


WHAT THE RENDERS SHOWED
-----------------------

`gen-owl --no-use-native-uris`, `gen-doc` and `gen-mermaid-class-diagram` all
exit 0. The OWL carries eighteen classes and all seven is_a edges, and the six
stated_rule annotations survive as triples. Three things worth knowing before
anyone looks:

The OWL carries the whole facts block as one 16,673-character string literal on
one line, because facts live under `annotations` and gen-owl renders annotations
as triples on the ontology. It was 12,176 characters at 127 facts and is now a
third of the TTL. It disappears when the draft is sealed — `seal` strips
`facts` — so this is a property of looking at a draft, not of looking at a
version.

The Mermaid diagram flattens is_a, so each of the five movement subclasses
repeats its parent's six relationships: twenty of the thirty-two edges are the
same six seen five times. Readable, but a change to StockMovement in v2 will
show up six times in the diff rather than once. `gen-mermaid-class-diagram`
writes one file per class into a directory and requires `-d`; `gen-doc` writes
84 Markdown files the same way.

The slot-level `unit` metadata does not reach the OWL at all. Kilograms, litres,
pounds and days are in the YAML and in nothing generated from it so far.
